How we approach Paid Advertising

Every market has a crowded ad auction — whether your buyers are in one city, one country or five. We build paid campaigns around the customers you can actually serve: a radius around a store, specific regions and languages, or global targeting for an online brand. Then we exclude the places, placements and searches that never convert for you, so budget goes where demand is real.

Paid advertising — explained simply

Paid advertising means paying platforms (Google, Meta, LinkedIn, YouTube) to show your message to chosen people — you control budget, audience and timing.

Example: A clinic runs Google Search ads for 'dentist near me' (people already searching) and Instagram ads showing the doctor and a consultation offer (people who weren't searching yet).

Google captures existing demand; Meta creates new demand. Results depend more on offer, creative, landing page and follow-up than on 'secret targeting'.

  • Start with one objective, one offer and tracking that counts real enquiries/sales.
  • Structure: prospecting + retargeting; 3–6 creatives testing different angles.
  • Judge on cost per qualified lead and CAC vs customer value, not clicks.

Common mistake: Boosting random posts, no conversion tracking, sending clicks to the homepage, and judging after 3 days.

Campaign structure — explained simply

Campaign structure is how you organise campaigns, ad sets/groups and ads so budget and learning go to the right place.

Example: Meta: 1 prospecting campaign (broad, 4 creatives) + 1 retargeting campaign (site visitors 30 days). Google: 1 campaign per service/location with tight ad groups.

Fewer, cleaner campaigns with enough budget each learn faster than many fragmented ones.

  • One objective per campaign. Separate prospecting, retargeting and brand where volumes allow.
  • Name things consistently so reporting is easy.

Common mistake: Splitting a small budget into 10 ad sets so none gets enough data.
