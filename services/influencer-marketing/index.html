Influencer & creator marketing — explained simply

Creator marketing pays or partners with people whose audience trusts them, so your offer reaches that audience with credibility.

Example: A café invites 5 local food creators (5k–50k followers) for a tasting, with a clear brief, a trackable code and permission to reuse their videos as ads.

Audience fit and trust matter more than follower count. Micro/nano creators often deliver better engagement and cost for local or niche brands.

  • Brief: audience, key message, must-say/must-avoid, format, CTA, deadlines, usage rights, disclosure (#ad/#collab — follow ASCI influencer guidelines in India).
  • Track with codes, UTM links and landing pages; compare cost per result with your ads.
  • Whitelist/boost the best creator content as ads.

Common mistake: Picking by follower count, no brief, no tracking, no usage rights, buying from fake-follower accounts.

UGC (User-Generated Content) — explained simply

UGC is content made by customers or creators in a natural, non-polished style — reviews, unboxings, try-ons, testimonials.

Example: Ten 20-second phone videos of customers explaining why they switched to your product, used as Meta ad creatives.

It feels native to the feed and builds trust — often outperforming studio ads.

  • Collect: post-purchase request with incentive, review prompts, creator UGC services.
  • Always get written consent for reuse in ads.

Common mistake: Scripting it so heavily that it looks fake; using customer content without permission.
