Analytics & tracking — explained simply

Analytics tells you where customers come from and what they do, so you can put money where it works.

Example: UTM-tagged links + GA4 key events (form submit, call click, WhatsApp click, purchase) + Meta Pixel/CAPI + a simple weekly sheet of leads → customers by source.

Without tracking you can't tell a working channel from a lucky week.

  • Define 3–5 KPIs tied to money (qualified leads, CAC, revenue by channel).
  • Connect CRM outcomes back to ad platforms (offline conversions) to optimise for buyers.
  • Expect gaps: privacy changes and cross-device behaviour mean no tool sees everything — triangulate.

Common mistake: Tracking page views but not enquiries/sales, or counting the same conversion in three tools.

Attribution — explained simply

Attribution is deciding which marketing touchpoints get credit for a sale.

Example: A customer sees an Instagram Reel, later Googles your brand and buys. Last-click gives Google all the credit; Meta's own report may claim it too.

Every model is a simplification. Use one consistent source for decisions and sanity-check with blended numbers.

  • Ask customers 'How did you hear about us?' — self-reported attribution catches word of mouth and social.
  • Run geo or holdout tests to measure incrementality for big budgets.
  • Watch MER (total revenue ÷ total marketing spend) as the business-level truth.

Common mistake: Adding up platform-reported conversions and thinking you sold more than you did.
