Content marketing — explained simply

Content marketing is publishing genuinely useful material so the right people find, trust and choose you.

Example: A home-interiors firm publishes cost breakdowns, before/after walkthroughs and a 'questions to ask your designer' guide — and gets enquiries that already trust them.

Good content answers real customer questions better than anyone else, then points to a next step.

  • Pillars: education, proof, story, objection-handling, behind-the-scenes, offer, community.
  • One idea → many formats: blog → carousel → reel → newsletter → WhatsApp broadcast.
  • Use AI for drafts, but add first-hand experience, data and examples.

Common mistake: Publishing for the algorithm instead of the customer; volume over usefulness.

Topical authority — explained simply

Topical authority means covering a subject so thoroughly that search engines (and people) see you as a reliable source on it.

Example: A physio clinic builds a 'knee pain' hub plus articles on runner's knee, ACL rehab and exercises — all interlinked.

Depth and interlinking beat scattered one-off posts.

  • Map the topic: core questions, sub-questions, comparisons, problems, how-tos.
  • Update winners regularly; prune or merge thin pages.

Common mistake: Writing about everything loosely related instead of owning a few topics.
