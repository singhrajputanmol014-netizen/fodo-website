How to structure a first paid-media experiment

A first campaign should be designed to teach you something, not to "go viral". Here is a structure that keeps the lesson clear and the cost controlled.

Home / Insights / How to structure a first paid-media experiment

By Anmol Singh, Founder of FODO · 27 August 2026 · 1 min read

A first campaign should be designed to teach you something, not to "go viral". Here is a structure that keeps the lesson clear and the cost controlled.

Choose one objective

Leads, purchases or bookings — one. Mixed objectives make every result ambiguous.

Choose one audience and one page

Pick the audience you understand best and send them to a page built for this offer. Changing audience, creative and page at once means you will never know what mattered.

Write a hypothesis

"We believe [audience] will respond to [message] because [reason]. We will know within [time/budget] if cost per [result] is under [threshold]." Illustrative thresholds are fine — the point is deciding in advance what success looks like.

Test creative, not everything

Run three to five creative angles against the same audience. Hooks and formats are where early learning is fastest and cheapest.

Set a learning budget

Decide how much you are willing to spend to learn, and stop there to review. Do not let the platform's "learning phase" become your budgeting strategy.

Verify tracking before launch

Fire a test conversion and see it land in your analytics and your inbox. Nothing is more expensive than a week of unmeasured spend.

Review with evidence

At the end, answer: which creative won, what did a result cost, what surprised us, and what is the next single change? That answer is worth more than the first campaign's results.
