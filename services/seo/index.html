SEO (Search Engine Optimisation) — explained simply

SEO is making your website easy for search engines to understand and genuinely useful for searchers, so you show up when people look for what you sell.

Example: A dental clinic in Dwarka creates a fast service page for 'root canal treatment in Dwarka' with prices explained, FAQs, doctor credentials and reviews — and starts appearing for those searches.

Three parts: technical (Google can crawl/index a fast site), on-page/content (pages that match search intent better than competitors), and authority (links, mentions, reviews). It compounds over months.

  • Start with search intent: informational (learn), commercial (compare), transactional (buy), local (near me). One page per intent cluster.
  • Topical authority: cover a topic thoroughly with a hub page and supporting articles, linked internally.
  • Technical basics: indexable pages, sitemap, clean URLs, mobile speed (Core Web Vitals), no duplicate content, schema where relevant.
  • Measure qualified organic traffic, enquiries and revenue from organic — not just positions.

Common mistake: Chasing rankings for vanity keywords, publishing thin AI-generated posts, or expecting results in weeks.

Technical SEO — explained simply

Technical SEO makes sure search engines can find, crawl, render and index your pages quickly.

Example: Fixing a site where every page was accidentally 'noindex', compressing heavy images and adding a sitemap can unlock traffic without new content.

It's the foundation — great content can't rank if it isn't indexed or loads painfully on mobile.

  • Check: Google Search Console coverage, sitemap, canonical tags, redirects, broken links, mobile usability, Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS).
  • Schema (Organization, LocalBusiness, Product, FAQ where appropriate) helps search engines understand entities — it doesn't guarantee rich results.

Common mistake: Obsessing over a perfect speed score while ignoring that key pages aren't indexed or have duplicate titles.
